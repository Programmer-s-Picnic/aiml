What is Artificial Intelligence?
Start learning from zero.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the field of building computer systems that perform tasks associated with human intelligence—such as understanding language, recognizing images, making predictions and planning actions. You do not need to know calculus or Python to understand the basic ideas.

How does AI actually work?

Traditional programs follow instructions written explicitly by a programmer. Many modern AI systems, especially machine-learning systems, learn patterns from examples. A trained model uses those patterns to produce an output for new input.

1. Collect data→2. Train a model→3. Evaluate it→4. Predict on new data→5. Monitor results

A simple example: filtering spam

Show a model emails labelled “spam” or “not spam”. It learns clues in words and messages. When a new email arrives, it predicts a label. Check predictions using unseen test emails: a model can make mistakes, and its performance may change over time.

Important: Not all AI uses machine learning, and generative AI can produce inaccurate or invented information.

AI, ML, deep learning and generative AI

The big field

Artificial Intelligence

Intelligent behavior in machines: reasoning, search, planning, language and perception.

A subset of AI

Machine Learning

Algorithms learn patterns from data instead of relying only on hard-coded rules.

A subset of ML

Deep Learning

Multi-layer neural networks learn useful representations from examples.

Creates content

Generative AI

Models produce text, images, audio or other content; many use deep learning.

Where do we use AI?

🏫 Education

Personalized practice, translation and feedback, with teacher oversight.

🏥 Healthcare

Helping analyze scans and records; expert review remains essential.

🚜 Agriculture

Crop-image analysis, irrigation planning and weather-based decision support.

💬 Language

Search, translation, summarization and speech recognition.

What should I know before starting?

Do I need to code?

No, not to begin. Python becomes helpful when you start building models.

Do I need advanced maths?

Start with averages, percentages and graphs; add algebra, statistics and linear algebra gradually.

How can AI go wrong?

Biased data, leakage, poor evaluation, privacy issues and hallucinations can cause unreliable outputs.
